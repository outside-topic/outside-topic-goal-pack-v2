EXAMINATION LITERACY

Cambridge & Edexcel Command Words Cheat Sheet

Command words tell you exactly what the examiner wants you to do. Misunderstanding a command word is the single fastest way to lose marks on an otherwise correct answer.

Command Word What It Asks Recommended Starter Common Mistake to Avoid
State / Identify A short, direct answer without explanation. “The … is …” Writing a long paragraph that wastes time.
Define The precise, textbook meaning of a term. “X is the … that …” Giving an example instead of the definition.
Describe State what something is like or what happens in sequence. “First … then … this shows …” Explaining why when only what is asked.
Explain Give reasons or causes; show how and why. “This happens because … which leads to …” Listing points without linking cause and effect.
Calculate A numerical answer with full method steps. Formula → Substitution → Answer + Unit Skipping working and missing units.
Compare Identify similarities and differences directly. “Whereas A …, B …” Describing each item in separate paragraphs without comparing.
Analyse Examine constituent parts and explain linkages. “This leads to … therefore …” Stopping at basic description.
Suggest Apply knowledge to an unfamiliar or novel context. “One possible reason is …” Repeating memorised facts that do not fit the scenario.
Assess Weigh up the relative importance of different factors. “The most significant factor is … because …” Providing no final reasoned judgement.
Evaluate Judge strengths and weaknesses, then reach a supported conclusion. “On balance … because … however …” Writing a one-sided argument without balance.
Discuss Present arguments for and against, then conclude. “On one hand … on the other … overall …” Ignoring counter-arguments.
Justify Support a choice or decision with quantitative/qualitative evidence. “This is correct because the data shows …” Giving an unsupported opinion.

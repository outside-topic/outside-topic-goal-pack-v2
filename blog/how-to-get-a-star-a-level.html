How to Get an A* at A Level: A Teacher’s 10-Step Plan

GOALPAc Faculty Subject Lead October 2026 8 min read

An A* is rarely about natural talent. The students who reach the top grade share a set of habits anyone can learn.

The 10-Step Plan

1. Turn the syllabus into a checklist. Rate every point red, amber or green and revisit monthly.
2. Learn the command words. “Describe”, “explain”, “evaluate” and “discuss” each demand a different answer structure.
3. Make notes you can test yourself on. Flashcards, question-and-answer columns, mind maps with blanks.
4. Use active recall daily. Close the book and write what you remember from memory.
5. Space your revision. Revisit topics after a day, a week and a month to defeat the forgetting curve.
6. Start past-paper questions early. Topic by topic from the first month of study, rather than waiting until the end.
7. Mark with the mark scheme, then read the examiner report. Learn exactly which phrases earn marks and which common pitfalls lose them.
8. Build a mistakes log. Record every lost mark, its cause and the fix; review weekly.
9. Practise under real timing. From three months out, sit full timed papers without interruptions.
10. Get expert feedback on extended answers. Evaluate against official mark-scheme levels, not just a score.

Weekly Study Rhythm (Per Subject, ~60–90 min)

Day Focus (per subject, about 60–90 min)
Mon–Thu Topic review, active recall, 5 past-paper questions
Fri Mistakes-log review and flashcards
Sat One timed paper section, self-marked with examiner report
Sun Rest, light review of weak topics

Common Myths Debunked

  • Myth: “Only geniuses get A*”
    Reality: Consistent technique, precise mark scheme knowledge and structured mistakes-log review beat raw talent every time.
  • Myth: “Past papers are only for the final month”
    Reality: Past papers teach examiner thinking from day one. Doing them topic-by-topic embeds the standard immediately.
  • Myth: “More hours always mean better grades”
    Reality: 75 minutes of active recall and timed problem-solving beats four hours of passive re-reading or highlighter decorating.

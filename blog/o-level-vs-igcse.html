O Level vs IGCSE: What’s the Difference and Which Should You Take?

GOALPAc Academic Directorate October 2026 7 min read
Quick Answer

O Level and IGCSE are both internationally recognised qualifications taken around age 16, and both lead to A Levels. Cambridge O Level is available in selected countries and typically assesses all students at one level, while IGCSE (offered by Cambridge and, as International GCSE, by Pearson Edexcel) is available worldwide and often offers Core and Extended tiers. Universities treat good grades in either as equivalent.

Every year, thousands of families face the same question at the end of Grade 8: should my child take O Levels or IGCSEs? The names sound alike, the subjects overlap and both lead to A Levels. Yet the differences in exam structure, flexibility and availability can matter a great deal for an individual student.

What is Cambridge O Level?

An international qualification for 14–16-year-olds, available in a group of countries where it has a long history, especially in South Asia and parts of Africa. Most subjects are assessed at a single level, so every candidate sits the same papers.

What is IGCSE?

Cambridge offers Cambridge IGCSE, and Pearson Edexcel offers its own International GCSE. IGCSE is available worldwide and is the most widely taken international qualification at this stage. Many subjects offer two tiers: Core and Extended.

Feature Comparison

Feature Cambridge O Level IGCSE / International GCSE
Availability Selected countries Worldwide
Tiers Usually single level Often Core / Extended (Cambridge) or Foundation / Higher (Edexcel, some subjects)
Grading Letter grades A*–E A*–G or 9–1, depending on syllabus and board
Subject range Broad core subjects Very wide range, including more options and languages
Coursework Mostly exam-based Mostly exam-based; some subjects offer coursework options
Progression A Levels, foundation programmes, diplomas Same

Do universities prefer one over the other?

No. Strong grades in either are treated as equivalent evidence of secondary achievement. Your grades, subject choices and later A Level results matter far more.

Five questions to ask:

  1. Which does my school or exam centre offer? Check with local British Council or approved centres.
  2. Am I a private candidate? IGCSE and O Level both have pathways suited for external entry.
  3. Do I want tiered papers? If a student struggles with top-tier questions, Core tier may provide a targeted route.
  4. Which subjects do I need? Ensure specific language or technical subjects are available on your chosen specification.
  5. When do I want to sit? Check exam series availability (May/June, Oct/Nov, or January).

Can you switch?

Yes, most easily at the start of the course; the content overlaps heavily, so a good teacher can map the gaps in a few weeks.
